# Meet Mica and Beryl, Amber's security crew

Every request that reaches your application is a stranger at the door. Before
your code does anything useful with it, two questions need good answers:
what exactly is this, and who sent it?

Amber's crew now has a member for each question. Mica reads what comes in.
Beryl decides who gets in and what they may do once they are inside.

Like Amber, Grant and Gemma, they are characters that name a responsibility,
so you can tell who owns a concern before you learn every package name. They
are not spokespeople, and they do not approve claims; the maintainers do.

## Mica reads everything

Mica is security's professional face: calm, exact and a little bookish, with
round reading glasses and a satchel of books. Parsing is reading, and she
reads to published standards, carrying years of lessons from real security
failures.

Her code is here today. Mica is a pure-Crystal parsing library with no C
parsing libraries underneath. Her released versions read XML 1.0 and 1.1
without libxml2: a streaming reader, a document model, a builder and XPath.
Every parse runs in time that grows in step with its input, under a work
budget and a deadline, and it never fetches anything from the network or the
file system while it reads.

Her regex engine is public and under review in the same repository, and it
is the reason Amber V2 apps can leave PCRE2 behind.
[Mica's launch post](/blog/2026/11/20/meet-mica-amber-v2-apps-no-longer-link-pcre2)
has the details and the numbers.

## Beryl decides who gets in

Beryl is the cloak and dagger. She rides a black motorcycle, wears a
full-face helmet that is the source of her abilities, and keeps her face to
herself most of the time. Her accent color is heliodor gold, and Mica's
glasses are the same gold, which tells you these two work together.

Beryl owns the side of security that makes decisions:

- **Policies.** Authentication and authorization, written as named policies
  that read like plain statements and are enforced at each step of a request.
- **Supply chain.** Dependencies, CI actions, images and toolchains pinned
  and checked by hash.
- **Secrets.** Kept out of source code and handed to the app by Mo, the
  secrets broker, named for her black cat.
- **Disclosure.** A private way to report a flaw, and advisories once a fix
  ships.

To be plain about where she stands: Beryl is a design in progress, and her
code is not public yet. Part of her job is already in the releases, though.
Apps generated by Amber CLI 2.0.11 pin Amber, Grant and asset_pipeline by
commit, and `amber new` checks each one's checksum in `shard.lock`. That is
Beryl's habit, written down before Beryl herself arrived.

## Why two of them

Reading input carefully and deciding what to allow are different skills,
and they fail in different ways. A parser that guesses lets a malformed
request through; a policy that guesses lets the wrong person through. Giving
each job its own owner keeps both of them strict.

So when a request knocks, Mica will read it cover to cover, and Beryl will
ask to see some ID.
